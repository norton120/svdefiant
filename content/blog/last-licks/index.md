The two weeks before *Defiant* slipped out of Windmill for good were the most productive stretch of the entire refit — which is to say, impending departure is an excellent motivator. Everything that had been languishing on the list suddenly had a real deadline attached to it.

### The Icebox, Actually Fixed This Time

The refrigeration situation I wrote about back in June — where the original tech left the system leaking for months, killed the compressor, and I had to order a whole new conversion kit — finally closed out when Isotherm came back in stock. The new system went in and, against all odds, worked on the first try. Cold drinks. In July. In Virginia. It felt like a genuine luxury.

### Plumbing: Closing Chapters

The electropooper got its new electrode plate installed, which was the last step in closing out what I'll charitably call the Great Sanitation Adventures of the season. The cockpit scupper also finally got cleared — it had been draining slowly enough to be mildly alarming, and a slow-draining cockpit in a seaway is the kind of thing you'd rather have sorted before it becomes urgent.

### Electrical

A few things came together on the electrical front at once.

The Victron DC-DC charger is now wired in between the starter battery and the house bank. The idea is to keep the house bank happy while the motor's turning, regardless of what the alternator decides to do on any given day. The breaker arrived just in time to get everything buttoned up.

The DC main bus bars got color-coded liquid tape — red and black — over the positive and negative terminals. They sit uncomfortably close to each other behind the panel, and one loose wire bridging those two posts underway would have been a very memorable problem. That's fixed now.

The ESP2 sensor board got a wiring overhaul. The original setup was running the low-voltage sender signals over the long cable run, which was picking up noise; the new config pushes 12v down the long run and steps it down to 5v at the ESP2 via a buck converter, with the sender signals running short leads directly into the board at the edge. Smaller resistor on the fuel sender, and the hall effect sensor contact issue got resolved as a side effect.

### Finishing Touches

The dinghy got her chaps — sewn fabric panels that protect the hypalon tube where it bears against the davit cradle. Cheap insurance compared to replacing the inflatable in a season or two.

The running light bases and the nameboard bung plugs got their final varnish passes. Four coats. A compact varnish kit got built out and stowed under the quarterberth too, because touch-ups don't stop just because you left the dock.

Then the lines came off, and we were gone.
