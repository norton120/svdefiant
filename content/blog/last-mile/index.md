Six weeks underway now. Before things get too far into passage posts, it seems worth documenting the sprint of work that got Defiant out of the slip in the first place. The escape post covered the departure itself — the jellyfish, the biblical mosquitoes, the terrible swell — but glossed over the pile of closed tasks that made any of that possible.

## Electrical

The DC electrical system picked up a few overdue upgrades in the final weeks. First, a Victron DC-DC charger wired between the starter battery and the house bank. The engine charges the starter battery through the alternator; the DC-DC charger then tops the house bank from there while we're making way. The breaker arrived late but everything came together and it's been working quietly ever since.

While I was behind the panel I finally dealt with something that had been nagging at me for months: the two main DC terminal posts are uncomfortably close together. If one ever broke loose and contacted the other underway, the result would be very bad. A coat of red liquid tape on the positive post and black on the negative gives them at least a fighting chance of not welcoming each other.

The ESP2 sensor board also got a long-overdue rethink. The original wiring ran the fuel sender signals over a long cable run back to the board, which was introducing noise. The cleaner approach was to move the ESP2 out to the senders themselves, push 12v over the 16/4 cable, drop it to 5v with a small buck converter right at the edge, and read the sender contacts locally. Less cable grief, better signal — and it incidentally fixed a flaky connection on the hall effect sensor at the same time.

## Plumbing and Systems

Defiant's electroscan — the electropooper in polite company — got a new electrode plate. It had been inoperative, which is a real problem when anchored anywhere that takes overboard discharge seriously. New plate in, operational unit out.

The icebox situation was less tidy. What I hoped would be a quick repair turned out to need a full system replacement. Nobody had Isotherm units in stock, so there was a stretch of checking inventory like a nervous sailor every morning while the departure window kept moving. It eventually came in and got installed, which means we have refrigeration underway. Non-perishables had worn out their welcome.

The cockpit scupper had been draining slowly for long enough that I had almost started considering it a feature. Cleared the line, and now the cockpit drains the way a cockpit is supposed to — quickly, completely, and without drama.

## Brightwork and Gear

The running light bases and the bung plugs in the nameboard mount points all needed varnish. Initial thin coat, then a few more full passes of Epifanes. I also put together a proper underway varnish kit that stows under the quarter berth, since the brightwork will need attention on passage and there's no point improvising a drip-proof setup at sea.

The dinghy got a set of chaps sewn up to protect the hypalon from UV and abrasion. Sewing projects on a boat are always an exercise in patience, but the chaps came out serviceable.

Finally, all the interior teak got a proper wipe-down with Murphy's Oil Soap. Not glamorous, not technically demanding, but the wood looks alive again and the cabin smells less like old refit.

Defiant is underway, and she left as ready as a boat her age can reasonably be expected to leave.
