There was a stretch in late July — maybe ten days — where _Defiant_ went from "plausible" to "ready." The departure story is in the last post. This is the quieter version: what actually got checked off in that final sprint.

**Electrical**

The DC-DC charger from the starter bank to the house bank had been on the list for months. The concept is straightforward — alternator charges the starter battery, DC-DC isolates the conversion so the house bank gets topped off while we're motoring without the two banks fighting each other. The breaker had been sitting in a parts bag since June. Once I actually committed to it, wiring it in was a clean half-day job.

The ESP2 got a meaningful rewire while I was in the electrical locker anyway. The fuel sender line needed a smaller resistor, which meant rethinking the layout. I moved the ESP2 out to sit next to the senders themselves — runs 12V over the long cable now, bucks down to 5V at the device. Shorter signal runs, cleaner installation. As a side effect it resolved the intermittent contact problem I'd been chasing on the hall effect sensor for weeks. Didn't plan for that one; it just got better.

One safety item I'd been putting off: the main DC positive and negative bus posts behind the panel are close together. Not close enough to arc on their own, but a ring terminal that worked itself loose underway and swung against the wrong stud would ruin an otherwise nice day. I coated them with red and black liquid electrical tape and stopped thinking about it.

**Refrigeration and Plumbing**

The Isotherm icebox conversion replacement came back into stock the first week of July. I ordered it, had it shipped to Windmill Point, and the swap went without incident — the old compressor had been breathing atmosphere for three months and was simply dead. Getting cold storage back was a genuine relief. It had been broken since June.

The electropooper got the new electrode plate installed and tested operational. The cockpit scupper line had been draining slowly for longer than I care to admit; turned out to be a straightforward blockage in the line, which cleared without any drama once I actually made time for it.

**Finishing Touches**

The running light bases and the nameboard bung plugs got four coats of varnish. The cabin teak got a Murphy's oil soap pass. None of this is technically interesting, but _Defiant_ looked well-maintained instead of perpetually mid-project when it was done, and that matters in ways that are hard to quantify until you're actually living aboard.

Ten days of focused work, and she crossed the line from restoration project to functional vessel. We're out here now.
