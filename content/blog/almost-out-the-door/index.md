The last few weeks have been the unsexy kind of boat work — the stuff that has to happen before the fun stuff can. _Defiant_ is almost ready to leave Windmill Point permanently, which means working through a long dock list of systems that need to actually function before we're more than an hour from a mechanic.

### Electrical

Three electrical items got closed out, all connected to the same anxiety: what happens when we're fifty miles offshore and something in the DC system goes sideways.

The most alarming one in hindsight was the DC main bus bars. The two main terminals behind the electrical panel were close enough together that a loose connection bouncing around underway could bridge them — which would be a very fast, very bad day. A coat of red and black liquid tape on each one respectively brought that particular risk down to something I can live with. The kind of thing you look at afterward and wonder why it sat that way for as long as it did.

The bigger install was a Victron DC-DC charger wired between the starter battery and the house bank. The alternator charges the starter, the DC-DC pulls from there to keep the house bank topped off — cleaner than trying to run everything off the alternator directly, and it means we're actually building reserves even at low RPMs. Parts took a while to arrive but once the breaker showed up, the install was straightforward.

Then there's the ESP2, the little sensor node that tracks fuel and fluid levels. The original wiring had the long run carrying the signal line back to the panel, which picked up enough noise to make the readings unreliable. Rewired it to push 12V out to the sensor end, drop to 5V with a small buck converter, and run the sender contacts directly into the ESP at the edge. Cleaned up the fuel level readings and also happened to sort out a grounding issue with the hall effect sensor on the water tank, which had been acting up since before I can remember.

### Sanitation

The electropooper had been limping along on a worn electrode plate — the part that does the actual electrolyzing of the holding tank contents. New plate is in, system is back to full operation. I will spare everyone the details of what replacing it involved.

The cockpit scupper had been draining slowly enough that standing water after rain was becoming routine. Cleared the line and it now drains immediately the way it should. An embarrassingly simple fix I'd been putting off longer than I care to admit.

### Galley

The icebox conversion was the item I was most dreading, and it earned that dread. The old compressor unit failed completely, and it turned out nobody had a replacement system in stock. Isotherm was backed up until early July, so there was nothing to do but wait. When the new unit finally came in, Louie got it installed without drama. Cold storage is back. This matters a lot more than it sounds when you're planning passages.

### Finishing Up

Four more coats of Epifanes went on the running light bases and the bung plugs in the nameboards — the teak that takes the most direct sun. Got the dinghy chaps sewn to protect the Hypalon on the trip north.

The interior also got a proper wipe-down with Murphy's oil soap, which sounds mundane until you do it. Turns out teak looks genuinely good when it's clean.

Three items still stand between _Defiant_ and the slip she's called home for over a year. Getting close.
