We've been underway for two months now. Before I stop writing about Windmill Point and start writing about wherever we actually are, there's one more post owed: a proper accounting of what got done in that last pre-departure sprint. The escape post is the story of leaving; this is the inventory.

### Electrical

The most meaningful upgrade of the whole push was getting a Victron DC-DC charger wired between the starter battery and the house bank. Previously the alternator charged the starter, full stop — the house bank only saw electrons from solar and shore power. The new charger routes current intelligently from the starter to the house bank while the engine runs, so motoring passages now actually contribute to the charging budget instead of just burning diesel to move. A circuit breaker component took a few weeks to source; once it arrived the wiring was clean and the system came up first try.

While I had the panel open anyway, I dealt with something that had been nagging at me: the two main DC bus bars — positive and negative — were sitting close enough together that I could imagine a loose bolt making a very bad day. Coated both posts with color-coded liquid tape, red for positive and black for negative. If one of those ever comes adrift underway I don't want to find out what happens next.

The ESP2 tank sensor module got redesigned while I was in there. The original layout ran the sender signal over a long cable run, which was causing intermittent problems with the hall effect sensor contacts. Moving the ESP2 physically next to the sender bank — and running 12v power over the long run, dropping it to 5v with a buck converter at the edge — fixed the contact issue and cleaned up the sensor reliability we'd been chasing.

### Plumbing

The cockpit scupper had been draining slowly since before the relaunch. That's fine in the slip and unacceptable underway. It's cleared.

The electropooper got a new electrode plate. The unit works by electrolysis; the plate is a consumable that eventually loses effectiveness and needs swapping out, which we'd put off too long.

The icebox was the longest-running and most frustrating job of the whole departure sprint. The original conversion unit was dead in a way that wasn't repairable — nobody had replacement parts anywhere — and we had to wait for Isotherm to restock a complete new system, which finally materialized in July. Cold storage now works. That's not nothing on a boat in August.

### The Small Stuff

Final coats of varnish went on the running light bases and the bung plugs on the nameboards, both of which had been sitting as raw teak since install. Raw teak in full sun doesn't last. Sewed proper UV chaps for the dinghy to protect the Hypalon through the season. Wiped all the interior teak down with Murphy's Oil Soap, which accomplished nothing structural but made the cabin smell like a tack shop instead of a marina.

_Defiant_ is now two months and a few hundred miles from Windmill Point. Annapolis is close.
