I have learned that the Sea Gods are always listening; anyone so arrogant as to say out loud "we will be at port by Monday" will be rightly smote with a bad impeller or an unexpected hurricane. On the water you make plans with nautical piety, showing respect by stating not what _will_ happen, but what you'd _like_ to happen - complete with alternatives lest the mighty sea have a change of heart. "We will try for port by early in the week, if we need to ditch we'll anchor _here_ or _here_, until we get a window." Reverence is not going without a plan - it is going with many plans, serving them up to the Sea Gods like a waiter with a dessert tray, and hoping they chose one of the options you've presented instead of ordering "everybody dies" off-menu. 

## The Non-Plan

This is my hope for _Defiant_ from now into Q2 2027 (yes, I still look at life in fiscal quarters. Occupational hazards are a thing).

### First, Underway Again

Before leaving New Bern, _Defiant's_ standing rigging needs review by an adult. That business with the snapped jib halyard could be nothing or it could be the reason my mast pops off in the middle of the Atlantic, so now's the time to get it resolved. I also have hopes to install the new PyPilot brain (move the old RayMarine to a fallback) so I can finally set the helm to follow tracks in OpenPlotter. It would be nice to fix the toilet leak (fresh water not gross water, but still) do a proper once-over of the motor. Ideally this all happens before December first. 

### ICW to Charleston, SC

Continuing down the ICW to Charleston gives Bash and I a chance to take some of the inside path at a slower pace. We've been sprinting since we left Annapolis, so a week or  so bouncing along anchorages, checking out towns, and taking a few breaks along the way would be a nice change. The Charleston leg will also give us a few places we need to tide-hop (timing our day so we catch high water across shallow stretches), and I didn't want to leave the ICW without having some experience playing that game. 

### Charleston Stage

Charleston seems like a good "last stop inside."  I can leapfrog the truck and motorcycle down from New Bern (it's only about 6 hours driving with stops), find a local delivery Captain that is able to go with ~48hr notice, and plan for a short, single overnight outside passage with a 36 hour weather window. In theory, at this moment, that is the ideal teeth-cutting for _Defiant_ and her crew into open ocean. 

Ideally Bash will catch a ride on land from Charleston to St. Augustine. There will be enough variables without including murder chihuahua in the mix, and if I can somehow relocate the truck, bike and doggo at once that would be perfect. The mechanics of such a move are still not clear. Alternatively, Bash will come along and I'll perform one last bike-truck hop to St.Augustine before we head out (adding yet another 18 hours of driving). As with most nautical plans, anything outside of a few days loses fidelity. 

less-than ideal would be one of a thousand reasons _Defiant_ could be unable to head out to see from Charleston: standing rigging trouble, hull damage, electrical problems - you don't know what will go until it does. In that case, progress south will likely be the long slow road on the ICW, or an extended stay for repairs in Charleston. Worse, we could run into motor trouble - I am adding senders this week so I can keep a closer eye on engine temp and oil pressure, and the engine will get a proper service before we head out again, but these things are far from predictable. Problems with the diesel could render all plans null and void rather quickly. 

### Outside

Hiring a local means a few easy wins: 

- I don't need to pay to transport a captain from somewhere else

- I don't need to pay for standby time until they are onboard

- I get someone that knows these particular inlets extremely well

- It will be cheaper and easier to get them back to Charleston when the run is over

Additional crew will be whatever it may be - I won't call extra hands a necessity myself. If she can do it at all, _Defiant_ can certainly make an overnight passage of ~30 hours with two able bodied sailors (or at least that's my thinking).  If we time the inlet at St. Augustine poorly, or weather/sea state surprises us, we'll ditch at St. Mary's and take a day on the ICW to finish the trip inland. 

### On The Ball

Once we are in St. Augustine, life slows _way_ down (and the plan resolution gets awful fuzzy from here, so take it with a grain of salt). Cosmetic projects (like the head refit and installing the hard top) can take priority. Maybe I'll finally get the damn bathroom faucet to stop leaking for good. It'll also be a good time to settle into the town a bit - take some trips on the motorcycle, spark some non-boat-related hobbies again, visit doctors and veterinarians and barbers and all the other routine things that get put aside on the water. Bash will have regular, proper walks again, before he turns into a fuzzy Orson Wells. 

### On The Hard

There is a well-known DIY boatyard about 20nm from St. Augustine, and late February seems like a good time to dig into much-needed refit work. _Defiant_ needs paint, and plumbing, and enormous amounts of brightwork. She has old thru hulls to be replaced and hardware to be rebedded. If I want her to be a better single-hand vessel then all lines need to be run to the cockpit. February => April '27 is admittedly a lifetime away from my current seat on the dock in New Bern; but from here, winter in Florida seems like the right time and place to get restorative work done. 

### Miami

Once _Defiant_ is all shiny and new, it just makes sense to sail her to _Vice City_. An offshore passage in April (or a string of bridge crossings inside) will get us to the Miami mooring fields for a few weeks - a proper send-off for _Defiant's_ first year of pilgrimage. 

### Northward Bound

Any sailing plan stretching half a year out is effectively a fairy tale; but it helps to have that far-off idea, just in case things actually work the way you intended. If by some miracle, April '27 finds Bash and I aboard a newly refitted _Defiant_ outside Miami, it will be time to start plotting our course north. I have no idea if I'll favor outside passages or stick to the ICW in certain parts by then, I don't even know **what** I don't know at the moment. 

### The Hazardous Journey

> *"Men wanted for hazardous journey. Low wages, bitter cold, long hours of complete darkness. Safe return doubtful. Honour and recognition in event of success."*
> 
> ~ Ernest Shackleton, maybe

Yes, I am aware that Shackleton most likely never ran that ad, and deconstructionists love yet another example where historic fortitude may be torn apart from the warm, safe cocoon of the present. Like most good quotes it does not matter if the source material is genuine - it matters that the message fits. 

This is a relatively tame endeavor in the grand scheme of things. Yes, most every single day offers genuine opportunities for participants to die - really die, not get their feelings hurt or get pwned in a video game. The risk is relatively contained and reasonably mitigated along the way; still, there is a great semantic distance between this experience and your average suburban weekend spent scouring Whole Foods for the most asymmetrical heritage squash that would be perfect in a fall table setting. 



Let's see how this journey goes.
