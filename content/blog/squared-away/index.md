The last post ended with us underway, but it skipped over the week of work that made "underway" feel like a realistic option rather than a gamble. These were the items that closed out in that final sprint before we cast off.

**Electrical**

The DC main posts behind the panel had been nagging at me for months — two fat bus bars sitting exposed, close enough together that a broken lug floating around in the bilge during a hard tack could be a very bad day. Red liquid tape on positive, black on negative. An hour of work I'd been putting off for the better part of a year.

The Victron DC-DC charger also finally went in. The breaker had been sitting in a parts bin since June; the install was straightforward once I stopped procrastinating. The setup pulls from the starter battery while we're motoring, supplementing solar for overnight passages when the sun is doing nothing useful. Simple redundancy that took an embarrassingly long time to actually get wired up.

The ESP2 got a proper rewire while I was already in the electrical bay. The original setup had the fuel sender resistor in the wrong location, and the long signal run was picking up noise. Moving the ESP2 out to the edge, pushing 12V over the long cable and dropping to 5V at the board with a buck converter, cleaned everything up. It also resolved a contact problem on the hall effect sensor that had been intermittently dropping fuel readings at the worst possible moments.

**Cold Storage**

Icebox was the one I'd been dreading. The original compressor conversion had never worked reliably, a repair attempt this spring confirmed it was beyond salvage, and the whole system needed to come out. Isotherm was backordered most of June and into early July. When the unit finally arrived and went in, it worked. Having actual cold storage underway has been one of the bigger quality-of-life changes on this trip — it sounds minor until you've been without refrigeration in July.

**Head and Deck**

The electropooper got a new electrode plate. These consumables don't last forever and neither does patience with a non-functional head. The cockpit scupper, which had been draining slowly enough that I'd started to think it was just the boat's personality, turned out to have a simple clog in the hose run. It drains properly now.

**Finishing**

Varnish went on the running light bases and the nameboard bung plugs. I also put together a compact underway varnish kit that lives under the quarterberth, so I can keep up with brightwork on the hook without improvising. The dinghy got chaps sewn for the hypalon — later than I would have liked, but done before any real damage set in.

_Defiant_ left the dock in better shape than she arrived at it. That doesn't sound like much, but after the year she's had, it's everything.
