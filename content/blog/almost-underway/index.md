The break after _Defiant_'s maiden voyage didn't last long. June and July were a two-month sprint to finish the departure checklist — the long, unglamorous kind of work that gets a boat actually ready to leave a dock forever rather than just leave it for an afternoon.

### Sails

The staysail is done, which is a bigger deal than it sounds. The sail came back from Evolution with the luff bolt sewn on, the furler went up (a bag-winched staysail underway is nobody's idea of a good time), and a pair of fairlead blocks now give the sheets somewhere sensible to lead. The jib sheets got rerouted inside the lifelines too — one of those things that was obviously wrong, had been wrong for a while, and took about twenty minutes to fix once I stopped ignoring it.

### Engine

Opening the Racor to check the primary fuel filter revealed what I can only describe as mud. Not just dirty diesel — actual mud, with texture and ambition. There's no satisfying explanation for how particulate of that character got into a fuel tank, but the filter went in the trash and everything got fresh diesel and bled air. The secondary filter was no prize either. The fuel sender had also quietly stopped sending at some point, meaning I'd been running on approximation and the dipstick, so that got pulled and replaced too. Motor checklist is now written down somewhere where I will actually consult it.

### Electrical

The house bank was always dependent on shore power or solar, which is acceptable at a marina and not acceptable offshore. A Victron DC-DC charger now routes a feed off the starter battery into the house bank while the engine is running — it won't replace the alternator but it adds a layer I felt good about having. The tank monitoring node also got a full wiring revision: it made more sense to put the sensor board out at the senders and push 12V over the long cable run, rather than pushing the weak millivolt sender signals over a run that picked up noise the whole way. While I had the panel open, the bare main DC bus terminals got liquid-taped. Those posts are close enough to each other that a chafe-through or loose cable underway was a genuine shorting hazard.

### Below Decks

The icebox has been its own saga. The R134a conversion done last year turned out to have a vacuum leak that wouldn't hold, and when that finally got traced and fixed, the compressor plate itself failed. The replacement Isotherm system was backordered until early July. It is in now and it is cold, and it was worth every week of waiting. The head got a new salt pump — the old one gave up mid-season — and a new electrode plate for the electropooper, which is exactly what it sounds like.

### The Rest

The [bow nameboards](/blog/nameboards) are finally on the boat instead of taking up cabin space. The exposed teak got its last varnish passes. Interior wood is oiled down. CG inspection is done and passed. The storage unit is empty and cancelled. Propane tanks are full, the truck is parked somewhere it can stay for a few months, the folding bike is lashed to the stern rail, and the cockpit scupper is unclogged.

The Leave Windmill milestone is one item from done. Annapolis is next.
