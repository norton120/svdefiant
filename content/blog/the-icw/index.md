Sometimes things happen fast. August took Bash and I up the bay to St. Michaels, where _Defiant_ lost helm control (in the middle of the town entrance, no less). 
Limped into Annapolis on autohelm, then September to repair the steering, go over the engine, and finally re-glaze the butterfly lights so they stop leaking.
October brough the Annapolis boat show, and immediately after... our voyage south began.


For the first week the bay was a meat grinder. A small craft advisory brought North winds between 20 and 30kts - all the rocket fuel we could want and headed in the right direction, but at odds with the sea. The wave periods were short and brutal, it was a fight to get twenty miles out of a day. What was more significant than the brutal seas and the mileage (or rather, lack thereof) was my state of mind. Two years ago I started this same trip down, ran into the same "witch of November" - the pounding North winds and grinding seas as the Earth rearanges the atmosphere in preporation for winter - and at the time my sophmoric knowledge of sailing was filtered through adrenaline-soaked terror. Everything about the line trim and the rudder felt clumbsy and numb. Adjustments that should have been smooth and linear came out rigid and binary.
