The [last post](/blog/escape) covered the escape itself — the jellyfish gauntlet, the 4am fuel dock drama, the swell that nearly turned me back. What it didn't get into was everything that had to actually close out before I could pull the lines. Here's the last-mile work that made departure possible.

### Power

The biggest electrical addition before leaving Windmill was a Victron DC-DC charger between the starter battery and the house bank. The logic is straightforward: the alternator charges the starter battery while the engine's running, and the DC-DC charger steps that energy across to the house bank. Combined with the solar array and the wind generator, *Defiant* should be able to run indefinitely without shore power. The breaker had come in back in June and it was wired and tested well before we left.

Behind the main panel I also finally dealt with the two big DC bus posts. They sit close enough together that a loose lug underway could mean an arc, and an arc at the DC mains is not a problem I want to diagnose offshore. Five minutes with a brush and some liquid tape — red on the positive, black on the negative — and that particular nightmare scenario is off the table.

The fuel sender ESP2 got a full rewire while I was in there. The original setup had a resistor sizing problem that was giving me unreliable fuel tank readings, and the long cable run made it worse. The fix was to move the ESP2 out to the sender itself, run 12v over the heavier 16/4 cable and step it down to 5v with a buck converter at the edge, then wire the sender contacts directly into the ESP2. Cleaner and more reliable. As a bonus it also resolved the hall effect sensor contact issues that had been intermittent.

### Head and Icebox

The "toilet part" mentioned in the escape post was a replacement electrode plate for the electropooper — *Defiant*'s electrolytic holding tank treatment unit. New plate in, system operational. That one was mercifully painless.

The icebox was not. What the issue tracker had optimistically described as "fix icebox conversion" turned into a full refrigeration system replacement when it became clear the original unit was done and no repair was going to save it. Nobody had Isotherm replacement systems in stock, so it sat open and warm through most of June. The parts came in the first week of July, Louie (our marina's excellent repair tech) came out and did the swap, and cold storage was finally back. I am deliberately not calculating what cold beer underway now costs per can.

The cockpit scupper had been draining slowly for months. Turns out drains work better without a solid plug of debris in them.

### Brightwork and Interior

The nameboard bung plugs and running light teak bases needed varnish before we left — exposed teak on a boat that's about to go offshore needs to be sealed or it'll absorb water fast. Five or six coats of Epifanes went on over the course of a few weeks. I also built out a proper underway varnish kit — everything needed to do touch-ups at anchor — which now lives stowed under the quarter berth for the passage south.

The interior got a full scrub with Murphy's Oil Soap. After a year of refit sawdust, epoxy fumes, and general chaos, the cabin is starting to look like a boat again rather than a construction site.

### Dinghy

Sewed a set of chaps for the inflatable. The hypalon is in decent shape but it's been taking more UV than I'd like; a proper fitted cover should buy it another season or two before I have to think harder about it.

*Defiant* is now a few weeks into the Bay and everything is holding together. The next big stop is Annapolis.
